# Strategy — news-summary-01

Note: per the kit's RULES.txt, the judge reads only the bytes of
`/output/entry.txt`; this file is not scored. It records why the summary
is built the way it is.

## Format

A numbered list, most important first, one or two sentences per story.
The house's own calibration (competition.yaml) ranks this shape highest:
its strongest reference rung is a ranked eight-item list, above the themed
and prose versions. A short dateline line fixes the day up front.

## Story choice and order

Eight stories, chosen for consequence and breadth across regions and
fields: (1) Abu Dhabi Russia–Ukraine talks, (2) Iran-linked plots and the
Bessent threats, (3) COP31 opening, (4) Houthi intercepts and Lebanon,
(5) Hegseth impeachment block plus the Mississippi Senate result,
(6) Congo Ebola, (7) markets and oil, (8) the Seongnam bus crash.
Diplomacy and security first, then climate, US politics, health,
markets, and one Asian item for geographic spread.

## Checkability

Every story names its outlet in parentheses (Reuters, AP, AFP, New York
Times, Yonhap), because the judge's third criterion rewards a source a
reader can verify. Every figure is one found in reporting that day:
a prisoner exchange agreed in principle, nearly 20 alleged plots,
102 heads of state and government, 11,000 Ebola cases, Brent at $92.76.
Allegations are worded as allegations ("charged", "alleged plots",
"said it intercepted"). Nothing is invented: inventing news is a
disqualifier in the rules.

## What was left out on purpose

Stories that happened or were first reported on 10 November, not the
11th — the US Army officer arrested in Britain, Israel's new parliament
being sworn in — even though they were still in the news on the 11th.
The brief is the 11th only. Unverified items were also dropped: an El
Salvador gang strike I could not verify because bbc.com was blocked
here, and a Nasdaq figure that would have contradicted the "Wall Street
slipped" line in the same sentence.

## Length

1,724 of the 2,000 allowed characters. Long enough to be specific,
short enough to read in a minute. Length for its own sake does not count,
so no padding.
